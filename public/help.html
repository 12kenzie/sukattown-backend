About SukatTown

SukatTown is an IoT-based energy monitoring system designed specifically for public schools. It tracks electricity consumption in real-time using ESP32 hardware sensors and displays data on a digital dashboard that helps school administrators identify usage patterns, detect anomalies, and receive AI-powered predictions for cost-effective power management.

The system aims to help public schools save energy, reduce electricity costs, and limit carbon emissions by giving administrators the information they need to make smart, actionable decisions about how their school uses power.

Real-time Monitoring
Live voltage, current, power, and energy readings from your ESP32 sensor every 5 seconds
AI Insights
Machine learning detects patterns, forecasts end-of-day usage, and provides energy-saving recommendations
Alert System
Hourly, daily, and monthly threshold alerts plus fire detection with Telegram notifications
Cost Projection
ML-powered billing estimates and monthly budget tracking with overspend warnings
Getting Started
1
Create Your Account
Visit the login page and register as a Principal to create a new school, or as a Teacher/Staff using your school's invitation code. Your account type determines which features you can access.
2
Set Up Your ESP32 Device
Flash the SukatTown Arduino sketch to your ESP32. Enter your WiFi credentials, school_id, and user_id from your account's localStorage (check browser DevTools → Application tab). The device will automatically begin sending sensor data to the backend.
3
Connect Telegram Notifications
Go to Settings → Telegram Integration. Message @userinfobot on Telegram to get your Chat ID, then save it in settings. You will receive instant alerts for power thresholds and fire detection.
4
Configure Thresholds & Budget
In Settings, set your maximum power threshold (Watts) and — for Principals — your monthly electricity budget. The dashboard will warn you when you approach or exceed these limits.
5
Invite Your Staff
As a Principal, go to Settings → Telegram Integration to find your school's invitation code. Share this with teachers and staff so they can register and join your school's monitoring group.
Your school_id and user_id are stored in your browser's localStorage after login. Open DevTools (F12) → Application → Local Storage to find them before flashing your ESP32.
Dashboard Guide

The main dashboard (index.html) displays live sensor data, cost projections, AI insights, and alert history. Here's what each section does:

Today's Consumption Tile

Shows cumulative energy consumption for the day in kWh, along with the day's peak and average readings. Data is fetched live from your ESP32 via the backend every 5 seconds. The value resets each day.

Voltage Stability Tile

Displays real-time RMS voltage (V), frequency (Hz), and power factor (PF) from your sensor. Nominal voltage in the Philippines is 220V. Power factor should ideally be close to 1.0 — values below 0.85 indicate inefficiency.

Projected Monthly Bill (Principal only)

Uses TensorFlow.js linear regression to project your end-of-month electricity bill based on current usage trends. The calculation uses a rate of ₱13.1734 per kWh. When fewer than 10 readings are available, it falls back to a simple daily average projection. Only visible to Principal accounts.

AI-Powered Insights

Updated every 30 seconds, the AI block analyzes your recent sensor history and identifies:

  • Pattern Detected — whether usage is stable, increasing, decreasing, or highly volatile
  • Insight — a quantified observation about your current vs average usage
  • Recommendation — actionable advice based on the pattern, including time-of-day awareness (e.g., peak evening hours)

Requires at least 5 data points to generate meaningful insights.

ESP32 Device Setup

The SukatTown ESP32 device sends power readings every few seconds to the backend. It measures voltage, current, power, energy, frequency, and power factor using a PZEM-004T sensor module.

1
Find Your school_id and user_id
Log in to the dashboard, then open browser DevTools (F12) → Application → Local Storage → your site URL. Copy the values for schoolId and userId.
2
Edit the Arduino Sketch
Open sukattown_esp32.ino and fill in your WIFI_SSID, WIFI_PASS, SCHOOL_ID, and USER_ID constants at the top of the file. The ESP32 must be on the same WiFi network as your router (it does not need to be on the same network as your PC).
3
Flash and Test
Upload the sketch via Arduino IDE. Open the Serial Monitor at 115200 baud to see connection logs. If successful, you will see HTTP 200 responses and the dashboard's connection badge will turn green within 10 seconds.
The ESP32 requires WiFi to function. If your school only has wired (ethernet) internet, a WiFi router or hotspot must be available within range of the sensor device.
By default, the sketch uses 180 seconds = 1 virtual hour for testing. Change this to 3600 in the sketch before deploying to production.
Frequently Asked Questions
What is the purpose of SukatTown?

SukatTown aims to help public schools save energy and limit carbon emissions by tracking energy consumption and displaying a real-time dashboard. It helps school administrators identify patterns or trends and provides predictions for cost-effective power management — guiding them to formulate actionable steps for energy management.

What exactly is SukatTown?

SukatTown is an IoT (Internet of Things) system which tracks electric consumption with a digital real-time dashboard that identifies trends or patterns, and makes predictions for efficient power management. It combines an ESP32 hardware sensor, a cloud backend (Node.js + Firebase), and a web dashboard with AI analytics.

The dashboard shows "Offline" — what does this mean?

The "Offline" badge means the dashboard cannot reach the SukatTown backend server. This can happen because:

  • The backend server on Render is spinning up (free-tier servers sleep after inactivity — wait 30–60 seconds and refresh)
  • Your internet connection is down
  • The ESP32 device has not sent any recent data

The badge turns green automatically once a successful API response is received.

Why am I not receiving Telegram alerts?

Check the following:

  • Your Telegram Chat ID must be saved in Settings → Telegram Integration
  • You must have started a conversation with the SukatTown bot first (send it any message)
  • Your school_id must be correctly set in your ESP32 sketch — alerts are filtered by school
  • Use the "Test Telegram Now" button in the demo controls on the dashboard to verify your connection
What is the difference between Principal and Teacher accounts?
  • Principal: Full access — can view the projected monthly bill, budget tracker, manage staff, set privacy settings, and receive all alerts. Creates the school in the system.
  • Teacher/Staff: Can view the real-time dashboard and consumption history, but billing and budget details are hidden. Joins a school using the invitation code.
  • Admin: System-level access to view all registered schools and their data (subject to each school's privacy settings).
How is the electricity cost calculated?

SukatTown uses a flat rate of ₱13.1734 per kWh for all cost calculations. This is a conservative estimate for typical Philippine electricity rates. The system multiplies your total energy reading (in kWh) by this rate to estimate your bill.

The projected monthly bill extrapolates today's usage to the end of the month using TensorFlow.js linear regression when enough data points are available.

How does fire detection work?

The ESP32 device includes flame sensor and smoke (MQ-2) sensor support. When a fire or smoke event is detected:

  • The ESP32 posts to /api/fire-alerts with the detection type (flame, smoke, or flame_and_smoke)
  • The dashboard checks for new fire alerts every 2 seconds and displays a red popup alarm
  • A Telegram alert is automatically sent to all registered users of your school
  • The alert auto-dismisses after 15 seconds but is logged in the Alert History panel
How does the AI and machine learning work?

SukatTown uses TensorFlow.js running directly in your browser (no server needed) for on-device ML:

  • Linear Regression — trains a dense neural layer on time → energy readings to forecast end-of-day consumption. Requires ≥ 10 data points.
  • Pattern Analysis — uses statistical methods (trend slope, volatility coefficient) to classify consumption as stable, increasing, decreasing, or fluctuating.
  • Anomaly Detection — uses Z-score analysis (threshold: ±2.0σ) against a server-computed baseline to flag unusual readings.

The Intelligence page provides full access to all ML outputs including regression charts, heatmaps, rolling statistics, and an activity log.

Can system admins see my school's data?

SukatTown includes privacy controls in Settings → Privacy & Data Sharing (Principal accounts only). You can individually toggle:

  • Allow Admin to View Consumption Data — whether system admins can see your school's power readings
  • Allow Admin to View Billing Estimates — whether cost estimates are visible to admins

These settings are stored on the server and respected by all admin-level queries.

My ESP32 is sending data but nothing shows on the dashboard

The most common cause is a missing or incorrect school_id in the Arduino sketch. The backend filters all data by school_id — if it doesn't match your logged-in account's school, the data is stored but never shown to you.

  • Check your school_id in browser DevTools → Application → Local Storage
  • Verify the SCHOOL_ID constant in your sukattown_esp32.ino matches exactly
  • Re-flash the ESP32 after correcting the value
Can I export my consumption data?

Yes. Go to the Consumption History page and click the "Export CSV" button in the top-right of the data table. This downloads all currently loaded readings as a CSV file containing: Timestamp, Voltage (V), Current (A), Power (W), Energy (kWh), Frequency (Hz), and Power Factor.

Use the filter buttons (Last 50, 100, 500, All Data) to control how many records are included in the export.

Glossary of Terms
Term Definition
kWh Kilowatt-hour — the unit of energy used for billing. 1 kWh = using 1,000 watts for 1 hour. Your electricity bill is calculated in kWh.
Power (W) Watts — the instantaneous rate of energy use. Equal to Voltage × Current × Power Factor.
Voltage (V) The electrical pressure in the circuit. Philippine standard is 220V AC, 60Hz. Stable voltage is critical for equipment protection.
Current (A) Amperes — the flow of electric charge. Higher current means more devices drawing power.
Power Factor (PF) A ratio from 0 to 1 indicating how efficiently power is used. A PF of 1.0 is ideal; below 0.85 indicates reactive loads (motors, fluorescent lights) wasting energy.
Frequency (Hz) The AC cycle rate. Philippines standard is 60 Hz. Deviations can indicate power quality issues.
IoT Internet of Things — physical devices (like the ESP32) connected to the internet to collect and exchange data.
ESP32 A low-cost microcontroller with built-in WiFi used by SukatTown to read sensor data and transmit it to the cloud.
PZEM-004T The energy monitoring module connected to the ESP32. Measures voltage, current, power, energy, frequency, and power factor.
Z-Score A statistical measure of how many standard deviations a value is from the mean. SukatTown uses ±2.0σ as the anomaly detection threshold.
R² Score Coefficient of determination — measures how well the regression model fits the data. 1.0 = perfect fit, 0 = no relationship.
TF.js TensorFlow.js — Google's machine learning library that runs in the browser. Used by SukatTown for on-device energy forecasting.
school_id A unique identifier assigned to your school when a Principal registers. Required in the ESP32 sketch so data is attributed to the correct school.
Invite Code A 6-character code generated for each school. Teachers and staff use this to register and join a school's monitoring group.
